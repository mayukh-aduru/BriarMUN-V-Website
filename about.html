About Briar Woods MUN

What Model United Nations is, why our club exists, and what it's actually like to show up to a meeting.

Our Mission

Preparing Falcons to think on their feet.

Briar Woods Model United Nations exists to give students a place to practice public speaking, research, and negotiation skills that classrooms rarely have room for. We simulate United Nations and crisis committees where every delegate represents a country or character, argues a position they may not personally hold, and works with (and against) their peers to pass resolutions before time runs out.

Whether you've never given a speech before or you're chasing your fifth gavel, there's a committee for you. We run weekly practice meetings, host an in-house conference each spring, and travel to a full slate of regional and national competitions throughout the year.

MUN taught me to disagree with someone for two days straight and still shake their hand at the end of it. — Briar Woods MUN senior delegate
Program Snapshot

Quick Facts

Founded: Student-run since the club's founding at Briar Woods High School

Meets: Tuesdays, 3:15–4:30 PM, Room 214

Open to: All grades, no experience required

Conferences: ~12 per year, regional through national

Sponsor: Faculty advisor, Briar Woods High School

How It Works

What a Falcon MUN season looks like

Step 01

Join & Train

New members learn parliamentary procedure and speaking skills in weekly meetings.

Step 02

Get Assigned

Officers match delegates to countries and committees ahead of each conference.

Step 03

Research & Write

Delegates write a position paper outlining their country's stance on the topic.

Step 04

Compete

Delegates debate, negotiate, and draft resolutions live at the conference.

Why Join

Skills that outlast the conference

Model UN is competitive, but the skills it builds show up everywhere — job interviews, class debates, college applications, and beyond.

Speaking

Confidence at the podium

Regular practice speeches and impromptu moderated caucuses build comfort thinking on your feet.

Writing

Persuasive, structured writing

Position papers and resolutions teach students to argue clearly and back claims with evidence.

Teamwork

Coalition-building

Delegates learn to find common ground with people who don't start out agreeing with them.
